# Search Console analysis — 29 September 2026

## Conclusion

Organic search traffic is growing strongly. The latest 28 reported days produced **2,910 clicks**, up **155.0%** from **1,141** in the preceding 28 days. Impressions grew **132.9%**, CTR increased, and average position improved. There is no evidence of an overall traffic collapse in this export.

The clearest commercial/product risk is concentration: **one page, `/en/printables`, supplies 85.2% of exported page clicks**. Protect its URL, worksheet intent, content and direct downloads while improving usability. Grow related printable resources before investing heavily in unrelated subjects.

This workbook ends before the 29 September release. None of its growth can be attributed to the redesign.

## Source and calculation boundaries

Source: `arabfingers.site-Performance-on-Search-2026-09-29.xlsx`, supplied by the site owner. The original workbook was read without modification.

- `Filters!A1:B3`: Web search, “Last 16 months”.
- Actual daily coverage: **27 April–27 September 2026**, 154 consecutive dates, `Chart!A2:E155`. No 28–29 September daily data is present.
- Pages, queries, countries and devices are **whole-export totals**, not separate last-28-day breakdowns. The daily chart supports period comparisons for the site, but not page/query winners and losers over time.
- CTR is calculated as total clicks ÷ total impressions. Period position is an approximate impression-weighted average of the exported, rounded daily position values.
- Dates are used as exported, without conversion to Algerian local time. Google documents daily reporting in Pacific Time.
- Daily, country and device totals reconcile exactly: **4,477 clicks and 53,401 impressions**. No duplicate dates or dimension keys were found.
- The Pages table sums to 4,503 clicks and 54,608 impressions. Page and property aggregation differ, so page shares below use the **4,503 page-click denominator**, not the daily total.
- The Queries table has exactly 1,000 rows, totalling 1,582 clicks and 21,642 impressions. It represents only **35.3% of total clicks**. It is not a complete record of searches: export limits and anonymised queries prevent complete reconciliation. Do not call the missing clicks bots or lost traffic.

Google explains these boundaries in its [Performance overview](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/7576553?hl=en) and [data discrepancies documentation](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/17010575?hl=en).

### An impression dip that did not mean lost traffic

September 1–7 had 566 clicks and 10,946 impressions. September 8–14 had 657 clicks and 5,744 impressions. Impressions fell **47.5%**, while clicks rose **16.1%** and CTR moved from **5.17% to 11.44%**. Average position improved from approximately 7.65 to 5.45.

This is consistent with a changing mix of search exposure, but this Web export alone cannot establish the cause or isolate AI effects. The subsequently supplied AI report shows impressions rising from 1,526 to 1,623 (+6.4%) over those same weeks. The Web impression dip did not coincide with declining AI impressions. This still does not establish which queries or features caused the change.

## Revised implementation priorities

1. **Protect `/en/printables`.** Keep its existing URL, H1, PDF intent, useful explanatory content and all pack links. Review the prepared preview before deployment. Keep direct downloads and preview images visible on phones. Promote the existing complete workbook clearly without forcing a bundle on visitors who need a single sheet.
2. **Deepen related printable learning paths.** First priorities are the workbook, numbers, harakat and first-word practice. Link relevant games after the primary download action. The newly implemented counting game supports the number pack, but its own search demand is not yet established.
3. **Improve existing near-page-one pages.** Work on first words, colours and the child-focused alphabet guide. Inspect page-filtered queries first. Add only missing, useful content rather than changing titles that already describe the resource accurately.
4. **Verify story discovery before creating more stories.** Check indexing, canonical selection and rendered links. The redesign already gives existing stories a clearer route from the homepage. That is a more testable first step than adding a large new library.
5. **Measure outcomes after deployment.** Track organic clicks separately from AI impressions and PDF/activity outcomes. The ad-layout change addresses observed shifts, but its revenue and engagement effects remain unmeasured.

No public-site deployment or Search Console settings change was made during this analysis. The original workbook remains unchanged.
